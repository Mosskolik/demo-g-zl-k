# Reader (okuyucu) accommodation rules in math exams and non-leading scaffolding: where the boundary sits for a voice math assistant

> Method note: In this session, direct page fetches failed for www.jcq.org.uk and www.washoeschools.net because the hosts are outside the allowed network. Following the session rules, no other sites were fetched. Every finding below comes from **web-search snippets and summaries** of the cited documents, not from reading the full texts. Quotes are as close to verbatim as the snippets allowed. Before citing any quote in formal work, check the wording against the original PDF.

## 1. What do official exam bodies say a reader may and may not do in math exams?

### Takeaway
Every exam body found takes the same position. The reader is a **voice for the printed text, not a teacher**. They read the text as it is (verbatim), repeat it when asked, and on request locate diagrams, tables or symbols. They **do not explain, clarify, paraphrase, define, interpret, add emphasis, or cue the answer**. Some bodies go further: UK STA (KS2) does not even let readers say the names of math symbols, and ÖSYM removes visually based and complex questions for candidates who use a reader.

### Cited Findings

**UK – JCQ (GCSE/A-level, *Access Arrangements and Reasonable Adjustments*, AARA)**
- The reader "must only read the instructions of the question paper and questions, and must not explain or clarify" (secondary copy of the AARA reader section). — [Scribd copy of Access arrangements](https://www.scribd.com/document/719605328/Access-arrangements); official document: [JCQ AARA 2024-25 (accessible)](https://www.jcq.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/JCQ-AARA-24-25_FINAL_accessible.pdf), [Pearson-hosted copy](https://qualifications.pearson.com/content/dam/pdf/Support/Access-arrangements/jcq-aara-accessible.pdf)
- A reader may help a visually impaired candidate find diagrams, graphs and tables, but may not give factual information or suggestions beyond what a sighted candidate sees on the paper. A reader may help retrieve information from a diagram **only when the candidate asks**. A reader "cannot explain or clarify any questions or instructions or explain or offer suggestions for any diagrams, graphs or tables". — [JCQ Regulations memory aid – Reader and Scribe (2016)](https://learningsupportcentre.com/content/uploads/2024/10/JCQ-Reg-Memory-Aid-Reader-and-Scribe-2016.pdf); [Guidance on the use of a reader for examinations](https://irp.cdn-website.com/893e6432/files/uploaded/Guidance%20on%20the%20use%20of%20a%20reader%20for%20examinations.pdf)
- A reader repeats the paper's instructions or questions only when the candidate shows a specific need, and reads the text verbatim, but only when the candidate asks. — [JCQ memory aid 2016](https://learningsupportcentre.com/content/uploads/2024/10/JCQ-Reg-Memory-Aid-Reader-and-Scribe-2016.pdf)
- Under the older guidance, a reader in Maths and Science must have "appropriate subject knowledge". — [JCQ memory aid 2016](https://learningsupportcentre.com/content/uploads/2024/10/JCQ-Reg-Memory-Aid-Reader-and-Scribe-2016.pdf)
- **Decoding (2019/20 onward):** "Readers may decode symbols and unit abbreviations in Mathematics and Science examinations for candidates who require this arrangement to access the examination". Examples include reading a superscript 2 as "two squared" and the ∞ sign as "infinity". Decoding "does not involve explaining when or how the symbol is to be used or describing the symbol". Centres must make sure readers are trained to decode accurately. — [JCQ Decoding guidance for readers (2019)](https://www.jcq.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/Decoding-guidance-for-readers.pdf); [Pearson copy](https://qualifications.pearson.com/content/dam/pdf/Support/Access-arrangements/Decoding-guidance-for-readers.pdf); [JCQ Notice to Centres – decoding symbols 2023](https://www.jcq.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/Notice_to_Centres-decoding_symbols_2023_FINAL.pdf)
- For candidates without decoding approval, the older rule applies: a printed ">" is **pointed to**, not read aloud as "greater than". The 2016 memory aid also says "2²" should not be read as "two squared", which the 2019 decoding guidance conflicts with. — [JCQ memory aid 2016](https://learningsupportcentre.com/content/uploads/2024/10/JCQ-Reg-Memory-Aid-Reader-and-Scribe-2016.pdf) vs [JCQ Decoding guidance 2019](https://qualifications.pearson.com/content/dam/pdf/Support/Access-arrangements/Decoding-guidance-for-readers.pdf)
- A computer reader (text-to-speech software) may read text aloud, but it must not decode or interpret the paper. — [JCQ Decoding guidance](https://www.jcq.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/Decoding-guidance-for-readers.pdf) (search summary)
- Breaching the reader rules counts as malpractice (third-party summary). — [Guidance on the use of a reader for examinations](https://irp.cdn-website.com/893e6432/files/uploaded/Guidance%20on%20the%20use%20of%20a%20reader%20for%20examinations.pdf)
- JCQ published a March 2026 update to AARA 2025/26. Whether it changed the reader rules was not checked. — [AARA March26 updates](https://www.jcq.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2026/03/AARA_March26_updates_FINAL.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Allowed (common to all bodies):** (1) read the text verbatim; (2) **repeat** it on request, in whole or in part; (3) **locate** where a diagram, table or symbol is, and read its title, labels and values in a set order; (4) speak numbers, variables and symbols precisely and consistently (JCQ decoding / Smarter Balanced; STA is stricter); (5) read the question silently first so the reading comes out fluent.
- **Forbidden (common to all bodies):** explain, clarify, paraphrase, simplify, define terms, interpret, translate, emphasize words the print does not stress, read in a way that cues the answer, offer suggestions about a diagram, explain what a symbol does or when it is used, provide extra material (e.g., counters).
- Applied to a voice assistant: "explaining the question" can only mean **making the question accessible** (reading, repeating, navigating structure, describing a diagram neutrally), not **making it easier to understand**. The STA rationale, that saying a symbol's name can inadvertently explain its function, shows that even word choice can be a hint. The assistant should read notation consistently and without interpretation.
- ÖSYM's approach is to change the question pool rather than describe diagrams. So for the Turkish context there is no official "neutral diagram description" protocol. The assistant has to borrow one from JCQ/Smarter Balanced (title, then labels/axes, then values, with no interpretation).
- STA conflicts with itself: it allows clarifying *instructions* (2024) but not clarifying *vocabulary* (2027). For the assistant, the safe line is to clarify the *mechanics* of the task (e.g., "you need to give one answer") and never clarify mathematical *content*.

### Gaps
- The full reader section text (5.5) of JCQ AARA 2025/26 and the March 2026 update could not be read (host blocked). Exact clause numbers are unknown.
- Official PARCC (Pearson), NAEP and ACT reader-rule wording was not found.
- No explicit ÖSYM/MEB wording along the lines of "the reader may not explain or hint" was found. The Turkish rules define the job as "reading" and restrict the question pool; no explicit list of prohibitions turned up. MEB (LGS/e-Sınav) reader instructions were not found.

## 2. How do readers handle symbols, expressions, diagrams and vocabulary in math?

### Takeaway
The rule is **precise, consistent, structure-preserving reading with no interpretation**. A negative number is "negative", a subtraction is "minus". Fractions are read as "numerator over denominator", with "end fraction" if needed. Upper and lower case are kept distinct. Tables and graphs are read in a fixed order: title, then headings/axes, then values. Terms are read but not defined.

### Cited Findings
- ETS: -5 is read as "negative five", not "minus five". "When a subtraction operation is involved, read the sign as 'minus,' e.g.: x - 5 should be read as 'x minus five.'" Complex fractions are read by numerator and denominator, adding "end fraction" if it is unclear where the fraction ends. Capital and lowercase letters must be distinguished. Lowercase letters side by side can be read as multiplication. — [ETS Guidelines for a Test Reader (PDF)](https://www.eu.ets.org/pdfs/disabilities/guidelines-for-test-reader.pdf); [ETS web](https://www.ets.org/disabilities/resources/test-reader-guidelines.html)
- Louisiana LEAP: the negative sign is read as "negative", not treated as minus. When the same item has upper- and lowercase variables, the case of each must be stated. — [LEAP 2025 Accommodations and Accessibility Features User Guide](https://doe.louisiana.gov/docs/default-source/assessment/leap-2025-accommodations-and-accessibility-features-user-guide.pdf?sfvrsn=edcf8d1f_0)
- Pennsylvania: "Fractions, including improper fractions, should be read as 'the fraction (numerator) over (denominator)'". For graphs: read the title, then the label and numbers on each axis, calling the horizontal axis "the bottom of the graph" and the vertical axis "the left side of the graph". — [PA Read-Aloud Guidelines](https://www.pa.gov/content/dam/copapwp-pagov/en/education/documents/instruction/assessment-and-accountability/pssa/accommodations/read%20aloud%20and%20scribing%20guidelines.pdf)
- Texas: "Variables in algebraic expressions and equations should be read aloud as the letters, but not the words, they represent." — [TEA Guidelines for Reading Aloud Various Types of Test Questions](https://txassessmentdocs.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/ODCCM/pages/3053224128/Guidelines+for+Reading+Aloud+Various+Types+of+Test+Questions)
- South Dakota: cm³ may be read as "cubic centimeters" or "centimeters cubed". The document notes that readers may vary their wording, so it does not mandate a single form. — [SD Read Aloud Protocol](https://doe.sd.gov/assessment/documents/read-aloud.pdf)
- Kansas: in its read-aloud script, numbers in tables and graphs are read only if the student asks. — [K-State/KSDE Activity 21-4](https://www.k-state.edu/ksde/alp/activities/Activity21-4.pdf)
- Michigan M-STEP has separate read-aloud guidelines for math and ELA. — [Michigan Math and ELA Read-Aloud Guidelines](https://www.michigan.gov/-/media/Project/Websites/mde/Year/2018/02/05/Math_and_ELA_Read-Aloud_Guidelines.pdf?rev=9d2da745eb754f6a8a5f8e56c3d3ff9b)
- JCQ decoding: superscript 2 is "squared" and ∞ is "infinity", but decoding never extends to "when or how the symbol is used". STA: symbol names are not said at all. Pointing only. — [JCQ Decoding guidance](https://qualifications.pearson.com/content/dam/pdf/Support/Access-arrangements/Decoding-guidance-for-readers.pdf); [STA 2026 TAG](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/key-stage-2-tests-test-administration-guidance-tag/key-stage-2-test-administration-guidance)

### Inferences
- For a Turkish voice assistant: "eksi" for subtraction and "negatif" for a negative number; "x bölü y", or "pay ... payda ...", with "kesir sonu" (end fraction) for complex ones; distinguishing "büyük A" / "küçük a"; reading "3x" as "üç x" (like the ETS side-by-side rule, without adding "çarpı" (times)); keeping parentheses as "parantez aç / parantez kapa" (open/close bracket). Consistency matters: the same symbol always gets the same word.
- Diagram/table template: "Bir tablo var. Başlığı: ... Sütun başlıkları: ... Satır başlıkları: ... İlk hücre: ... satırı, ... sütunu, değer ..." ("There is a table. Title: ... Column headings: ... Row headings: ... First cell: row ..., column ..., value ..."). After that, give values on request. **Never** use a comparison or trend sentence such as "the graph is rising" or "the largest value is ...". That is interpretation.
- Terms such as "asal sayı" (prime number) or "çevre" (perimeter) are read but not defined. If the student asks, the assistant says "Soruda bu terim geçiyor; tanımını veremiyorum" ("This term is in the question; I can't give its definition") and offers to repeat it or spell it out.

### Gaps
- No official Turkish (MEB/ÖSYM) speaking standard for math was found. Turkish norms for reading math aloud (e.g., the Turkish braille math code, MEB "Matematik Braille" guides) are outside this note's scope and need checking.
- The Smarter Balanced "Mathematics Read Aloud" appendix with example expressions (e.g., the full table of exponents, roots and inequalities) could not be read in full.

## 3. What does the research say about scaffolding, productive struggle and hint design?

### Takeaway
Tutoring research sets the boundary on a continuum. At one end are **understanding-phase / metacognitive prompts** that leave the solution to the student ("What is the unknown? What is given? What is the condition?"). At the other end are **bottom-out hints** that give the next step or the answer. In the research, "focusing" questions are recommended and "funneling" questions are not. Recent LLM-tutor research shows that answers tend to "leak" over a multi-turn conversation under pressure ("scaffolding collapse"), and that prompting alone is not enough to prevent it.

### Cited Findings
- **Polya, understanding phase:** "What is the unknown? What are the data? What is the condition?" Then: "Is it possible to satisfy the condition? Is the condition sufficient to determine the unknown? Or is it insufficient? Or redundant? Or contradictory?" and "Separate the various parts of the condition. Can you write them down?" — [Polya How to Solve It summary (HMC)](https://www.cs.hmc.edu/~keller/how_to_solve_it.html); [UCR PDF](https://math.ucr.edu/~res/math133/polya.pdf); [Harvard Knill](https://people.math.harvard.edu/~knill/teaching/math21b2001/polya/index.html)
- **NCTM *Principles to Actions*:** "funneling" means using a set of questions to lead students to a desired procedure or conclusion while giving limited attention to responses that veer off. "Focusing" means "paying attention to what the students are thinking, pressing them to communicate their thoughts clearly, and expecting them to reflect on their thoughts and those of their classmates" (p. 37). Productive struggle support: "Giving students time to struggle, and asking questions that scaffold students' thinking without stepping in" (around p. 52, via conference slides). — [NCTM Types of Questions (session slides)](https://www.nctm.org/uploadedFiles/Conferences_and_Professional_Development/Institutes/Supporting_Students_Productive_Struggle/Session5%20-%203-5.pdf); [Principles to Actions ebook](http://areaiihsmap.pbworks.com/w/file/fetch/109255672/Principles.To.Actions.ebook.pdf); [Colorado DOE Effective Mathematics Teaching Practices](https://ed.cde.state.co.us/comath/effective-mathematics-teaching-practices); [NCTM Productive Struggle](https://www.nctm.org/Conferences-and-Professional-Development/Professional-Services/Productive-Struggle/)
- **IMPROVE (Mevarech & Kramarski 1997):** four kinds of metacognitive questions: comprehension, connection, strategic and reflection. The comprehension questions are meant to make students reflect on the problem *before* solving it. One secondary guide phrases them as "What is this problem actually asking? What information do I have? What am I trying to find?" The approach had positive effects on problem solving, especially on explaining mathematical reasoning. However, the original study combined three components (metacognitive training, cooperative learning, feedback), so the effect cannot be credited to the questions alone. — [Kramarski & Mevarech 2003 PDF](https://ssrlsig.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/06/kramarski-mevarech-2003-enhancing-mathematical-reasoning-effects-of.pdf); [WWC Study 79859](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/WWC/Study/79859); [Structural Learning guide (secondary)](https://www.structural-learning.com/post/metacognition-mathematics-teachers-guide); [Ollie Lovell critique](https://www.ollielovell.com/metacognition-can-help-students-problem-solve/)
- **Hints in ITS (Aleven & Koedinger):** in the Geometry Cognitive Tutor, students spent under a second on 68% of pre-final hint levels, clicking through to the bottom-out hint ("executive help seeking"). The authors call this over-focus on bottom-out hints undesirable. — [Aleven & Koedinger 2001](http://pact.cs.cmu.edu/pubs/Aleven%20Koedinger%2001.pdf)
- A more nuanced view: Shih, Koedinger & Scheines (2008) found that spending a long time on a bottom-out hint correlates positively with learning. Bottom-out hints help students keep going when stuck, and may help learning when spontaneously self-explained. — [Aleven et al. 2016, "Help Helps, But Only So Much" (IJAIED)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40593-015-0089-1); [CMU PDF](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~aleven/Papers/2016/Aleven_etal_IJAIED2016-Helpseeking.pdf)
- Metacognitive feedback on help seeking (Roll et al.): the feedback group went to the bottom-out hint in only 45% of hint sequences, fewer than the comparison group. — [Roll et al., Learning and Instruction](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0959475210000538); [PDF](https://pact.cs.cmu.edu/koedinger/pubs/Roll%20JLI.pdf)
- "Assistance dilemma": the trade-off between giving and withholding information. — [Koedinger & Aleven 2007](https://pact.cs.cmu.edu/pubs/Koedinger%20Aleven%2007.pdf); [HelpNeed Predictor (arXiv)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.04124)
- **LLM tutors:** MathTutorBench (Macina et al., 2025) scores "answer leakage" as a negative. EduFair-Bench defines leakage as whether the tutor "withholds the correct answer or key solution steps". — [EduFair-Bench (arXiv)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.12949)
- "Scaffolding collapse": under sustained student pressure, a Socratic tutor gradually drops guided inquiry and reveals solutions directly. — [Mitigating Scaffolding Collapse in Socratic Tutors (arXiv)](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.19371v1)
- In adversarial student simulations, "contextual manipulation" produced the highest average leakage rate. Prompt-only safeguards are weak. — [Evaluating Answer Leakage Robustness of LLM Tutors (arXiv)](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.18660)
- Defences include a judge plus refiner multi-agent setup, inference-time screening (SocraGate), and prompt optimization that treats leakage as a revision signal. — [SocraGate (MDPI Applied Sciences)](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/16/21/10561); [LLMs Are Already Good Tutors (arXiv)](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.27088)
- In scaffolding assessment, "response length, fluency, formatting, or authoritative tone are not evidence of scaffolding". — [Rethinking Scaffolding in LLM Tutors (arXiv)](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.15766)

### Inferences
- For "explaining the question with no hint", the safe zone is the **front half of Polya's understanding phase**: the assistant *asks* or *repeats*; it never *tells*. Example non-leading prompts (Turkish, with English glosses):
  - "Soruyu tekrar okumamı ister misin? Tamamını mı, bir kısmını mı?" (Shall I read the question again? All of it, or part of it?) *(reader-compatible)*
  - "Soruda verilen bilgileri sırayla tekrar edeyim mi?" (Shall I repeat the given information in order?) *(only the question's own sentences, in its own order)*
  - "Senin anladığın kadarıyla soru ne istiyor?" (As you understand it, what is the question asking for?) *(the student says it, the assistant does not correct the content)*
  - "Hangi bilgileri biliyorsun? Neyi bulman isteniyor?" (What information do you have? What are you asked to find?) *(Polya / IMPROVE comprehension)*
  - "Şu ana kadar ne düşündüğünü anlatır mısın?" (Can you tell me what you've thought so far?) *(focusing / self-explanation)*
  - "Bir şeyi not etmek ister misin? Söylediğin bilgiyi senin için tekrar edebilirim." (Do you want to note something down? I can repeat what you said back to you.) *(working-memory support, not math content)*
- **Forbidden prompt types** (funneling, i.e. hints): naming a strategy ("try drawing a table", "set up an equation"), a sub-goal ("first find the ...", "start with the area"), a relevant formula or concept ("remember the Pythagorean theorem"), cueing a key word or quantity through emphasis or ordering, hinting at an answer choice, giving right/wrong feedback on the student's intermediate step ("yes, that's right" is also a hint), calling a piece of information "important" or "unnecessary", Polya's "Do you know a related problem?" (this belongs to the *planning* phase and is a hint).
- Even Polya's "Is the condition sufficient? Redundant?" question can steer the student toward "there is a distractor in the question". That makes it borderline for exam mode. Better to leave it to the student.
- A design implication from the LLM research: "don't give hints" in the system prompt is **not enough**. A separate answer-leak checker (judge) layer and resistance to pressure across turns ("just tell me the answer", "my teacher allows it") must be added. Counting leaks in testing is recommended.

### Gaps
- No study was found that tests ITS hint design specifically with **blind students** or in a **voice-only** setting.
- There is no direct experimental evidence on how much non-leading Polya-style comprehension prompts alone (separate from other components) contribute to learning (IMPROVE is a combined intervention).
- The full MathTutorBench paper was not read. Its leakage metric definition comes from the EduFair-Bench snippet.

## 4. What guidance is there for TVIs (teachers of the visually impaired) on letting blind students do the math themselves, and on learned helplessness?

### Takeaway
Professional bodies for visual impairment name learned helplessness as a major concern, and they identify excessive support and prompting from adults, especially paraprofessionals, as a cause. The shared rule is: if the student can do it themselves, they do it. Prompt only when needed. Fade support over time. The TVI sets the expectations.

### Cited Findings
- NFB's guide for paraprofessionals working with blind students sets the goal of avoiding learned helplessness in the student. The first person a paraprofessional should turn to for guidance is the TVI. Treva Olivero: some paraprofessionals "will do things for blind students and not let them be independent", and many see their role as caretaker. — [NFB: Helpful Hints for Paraprofessionals](https://nfb.org/helpful-hints-paraprofessionals-working-students-who-are-blind-or-visually-impaired)
- Tennessee VIVA session: learned helplessness is intensified by professionals and caregivers who give excessive support and prompting. — [TN VIVA: Learned Helplessness (TSB)](https://www.tsbtigers.org/apps/video/watch.jsp?v=389905)
- Learned helplessness is "a major concern of professionals who work with people with visual impairments". — [Illinois State University thesis](https://ir.library.illinoisstate.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=2032&context=etd)
- Students should attempt tasks even when they are hard or slow. Prompt only when needed and reinforce effort and independence. Support new tasks with methods such as hand-under-hand guidance and reduce it gradually. — [TeachingVisuallyImpaired.com: Responsibility and Independence](https://www.teachingvisuallyimpaired.com/responsibility--independence.html)
- "If there is a way for them to do it on their own, they should be doing it on their own." — [Challenge Solutions: Tips for Paraprofessionals](https://challengesolutions.org/2020/11/28/tips-for-paraprofessionals-with-blind-and-visually-impaired-students/)
- NBA paraprofessional training: "You are facilitating independence!" — [National Braille Association: Basics 101 for Paraprofessionals](https://www.nationalbraille.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/Basics-101-for-Paraprofessionals.pdf)

### Inferences
- The voice assistant's default behaviour should be "I make it accessible, you solve it": repeat and navigate on request, never do a calculation or solution step for the student.
- An answer such as "I can't help you solve this, but I can read it again" is consistent with both exam rules and anti-learned-helplessness guidance, but it should be framed with encouragement ("Take your time, I'm here"). Encouragement must not depend on whether the answer is right.
- An offer of help that the student did not ask for counts as "excessive prompting". The assistant should not start talking on its own; it should wait for the student to ask.

### Gaps
- No relevant Paths to Literacy / Perkins article on learned helplessness specifically **in math** was found. The search did not reach those sites.
- No Turkish (MEB Özel Eğitim) guidance on learned helplessness for TVIs or special-education teachers was found.
- Evidence that assistive technology (voice assistants) could increase or reduce learned helplessness was not found.

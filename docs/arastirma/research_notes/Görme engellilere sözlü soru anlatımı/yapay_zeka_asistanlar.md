# AI, LLM, VLM and Voice Assistants for Blind/Low-Vision (BLV) Students in Math/STEM (2020–2026)

> **Method note for the report writer:** In this session, full-text fetching was blocked for arxiv.org, dl.acm.org, researchgate.net and ncbi.nlm.nih.gov because those hosts are not on the network allowlist. So every finding below comes from web-search result summaries and snippets of the listed pages, not from reading the papers in full. Exact numbers are labelled where they came second-hand. Labels used: **[PR]** = peer-reviewed (ACM CHI/ASSETS, PNAS, EMNLP, MDPI); **[Preprint]** = arXiv, not yet peer-reviewed; **[Product]** = vendor or company claim; **[Grey]** = reviews, trade press, forums, conference session listings.

## 1. Which HCI/accessibility studies evaluated AI image description or LLM tutoring for blind users in math/STEM, and what did they find on accuracy, trust, verbosity and user control?

### Takeaway
Many recent CHI/ASSETS studies look at how BLV people use general-purpose multimodal LLM describers (Be My AI, Seeing AI, ChatGPT, custom GPT-4o apps). They agree on four points: accuracy is mediocre on complex or detailed tasks, users actively verify outputs and do not fully trust them, descriptions are often long and miss the user's actual goal, and users want to interrogate the output through follow-ups and contest it. I found **no peer-reviewed study that evaluates an LLM math tutor or AI image describer specifically with blind students solving math problems.** The closest work is on charts and data visualizations, plus teacher-perspective and case studies.

### Cited Findings
**General MLLM visual-interpretation studies (BLV users)**
- **[PR] CHI '26, honorable mention.** Gonzalez Penuela, Jung, Lin, Hu and Azenkot, "How Multimodal Large Language Models Support Access to Visual Information: A Diary Study With Blind and Low Vision People." Design: a 2-week diary study with 20 BLV participants (13 blind, 7 low vision) using a GPT-4o-based app called VisionPal, October–December 2024. The arXiv version reports 551 diary entries; an earlier late-breaking version reported 553. — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3772318.3793266); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.13469); [Cornell Chronicle](https://news.cornell.edu/stories/2026/04/ai-tools-help-vision-impaired-are-good-could-be-better)
- Same study: the app worked well for general "What is this?" questions but struggled with detailed help on complex tasks and with detailed follow-up queries. A secondary summary reports **56.6% accuracy and 22.2% false information** in responses. These figures are second-hand and should be checked against the full paper. — [TechXplore](https://techxplore.com/news/2026-04-ai-tools-vision-impaired-good.html); [Cornell Chronicle](https://news.cornell.edu/stories/2026/04/ai-tools-help-vision-impaired-are-good-could-be-better)
- The earlier late-breaking-work version of the same study was published as a CHI '25 extended abstract: "Towards Understanding the Use of MLLM-Enabled Applications for Visual Interpretation by Blind and Low Vision People." — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3706599.3719714)
- **[PR] ASSETS '24.** Adnin and Das, "'I look at it as the king of knowledge': How Blind People Use and Understand Generative AI Tools." Design: interviews with 19 blind people who use ChatGPT, Copilot, Gemini, Claude and Be My AI. Participants doubted some outputs; for example, several doubted Be My AI's description of a dog's breed. — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3663548.3675631); [AFB presentation](https://afb.org/sites/default/files/2024-10/6.%20Das%20Presentation.pdf)
- **[PR] ASSETS '24.** Alharbi, Lor, Herskovitz, Schoenebeck and Brewer, "Misfitting With AI: How Blind People Verify and Contest AI Errors." Design: interviews with 26 blind participants about Be My AI (GPT-4), Seeing AI, Be My Eyes and similar tools. Participants often hit errors when reading **complex layouts**. Errors were worse for non-English and non-Western materials and for people with marginalized identities. — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3663548.3675659); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.06546); [NSF PAR](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10557171-misfitting-ai-how-blind-people-verify-contest-ai-errors)
- **Verification strategies** in the same study: participants test tools in low-risk situations to learn their limits, use nonvisual sensemaking, cross-check with other apps and devices, and sometimes ask a sighted person. — [author PDF](https://jayl.in/papers/2024_ASSETS_misfitting.pdf); [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3663548.3675659)
- **Verbosity and goal mismatch:** a University of Maryland study found that users often received lengthy descriptions that did not address their immediate goal. **[Grey]**: the source is trade-press coverage of the study. — [Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/cool-tech/ai-apps-blind-users-umd-study/)

**Charts and data visualization (the closest STEM evidence)**
- **[PR] ASSETS '24.** "MAIDR Meets AI." Blind participants interpreted charts with multimodal LLMs. At least one participant preferred concise responses in a table layout, which points to a tension between detail and usability. — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3663548.3675660)
- **[Preprint / EuroVis '24] VizAbility.** An LLM question-answering layer on top of keyboard navigation of charts with a screen reader. The user study was small and preliminary: 6 BLV participants from the National Institute of the Blind. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.09611); [arXiv HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.09611v3)
- **[PR, CHI '26 extended abstract] GraphWhisper, "Making Charts Speak."** A conversational LLM for chart question answering that extracts data directly from chart images. It self-reports 98.1% on visualization-literacy benchmarks and about 94% accuracy on 185 questions from 15 BLV participants. The method for scoring hallucinations was not visible in the snippet. — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3772363.3799030)
- **[Preprint / VIS 2026] "Touching or Chatting."** Compares LLMs with tactile charts. BLV users often want to know the **chart type first**, and they report higher cognitive load when interpreting LLM answers about unfamiliar charts. — [TU Graz PDF](https://visdesignlab.tugraz.at/papers/2026_vis_tactile-llm.pdf); [Utah PDF](https://publications.sci.utah.edu/publications/He2026c/2607.23065v1.pdf)
- **[Preprint] "Sensing the Shape of Data."** Non-visual exploration of histograms and statistical concepts with BLV learners. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.14452)
- **[Preprint] "Querying Multimodal Scientific Papers with AI."** Practices and preferences of blind, low-vision and sighted scientists when they ask AI about papers. Only the title was visible. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.18514)

**Education and math specifically**
- **[PR] ASSETS '26.** "Towards Accessible AI Literacy: Investigating How Teachers of Blind and Low-Vision Students Integrate Generative AI in Pedagogical Practices." Teachers worry that students who rely heavily on GenAI will not build core accessibility skills. A math teacher who uses a screen reader recommended ChatGPT to help students fill gaps in understanding visual concepts. — [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3797867.3829007); [author PDF](https://maitraye.github.io/files/papers/BLV_AI_literacy_ASSETS26.pdf)
- **[PR, ASEE conference] "Impact of Generative AI Technologies on Blind and Visually Impaired Students: A Case Study."** Visually intensive subjects such as math remain barriers, and GenAI is "not yet a complete solution." — [ASEE PEER](https://peer.asee.org/impact-of-generative-ai-technologies-on-blind-and-visually-impaired-students-a-case-study.pdf)
- **[PR, venue unverified] MathVision.** An accessible intelligent agent that converts equations to speech for visually impaired people. In a small test with students at schools for the blind, 9 of 10 equations were converted correctly. This is a tiny-sample prototype. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/386594728_MathVision_An_Accessible_Intelligent_Agent_for_Visually_Impaired_People_to_Understand_Mathematical_Equations)
- **[Grey] ATIA session "Math Inclusion and Solutions for Blind-Low Vision Using AT with AI."** Covers the pros and cons of ChatGPT, Khanmigo and Bing AI for math instruction, plus Word Math Editor and LaTeX. This is a session listing, not a study. — [ATIA/PathLMS](https://www.pathlms.com/atia/courses/74780)
- **[Grey/Product] Perkins on MathKicker.ai.** Describes a free tool that converts math expressions into accessible worksheets. No formal accuracy data. — [Perkins](https://www.perkins.org/resource/create-accessible-digital-math-with-mathkicker-ai/)
- **[PR] IES/ERIC technical brief "Using AI to improve math…".** Students with visual impairments struggle with test items that have complex text, long descriptions, design barriers and multiple parts. — [ERIC PDF](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED623510.pdf)

**Products: what has been tried**
- **[Grey] Be My AI** launched in March 2023 and is based on OpenAI's GPT-4. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Be_My_Eyes)
- **[Grey] Seeing AI:** a Common Sense Education review notes some accuracy issues; not all text is readable, and nonlinear text may be read out of order. This matters for math layouts such as fractions, matrices and columns. — [Common Sense Education](https://commonsense.org/education/app/seeing-ai)
- **[Preprint] Seeing AI** gave the most accurate text recognition among assistive apps in one 2024 accessibility evaluation, including in average lighting. That evaluation was of prose text, not equations. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.17496)
- **[Preprint] ChatGPT live video chat** was probed for real-world assistance for BVI users, and the paper documents gaps. Only the title and abstract were visible. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.03651)
- **[Preprint] BLV software professionals** describe GenAI as a "Game Changer" or an "Overenthusiastic Drunk Acquaintance," which captures the mix of usefulness and unreliability. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.24462)

### Inferences
- The evidence base is about **general visual interpretation and charts**, not about **math problem statements or equations read aloud to blind students**. Any design for spoken math explanation has to carry findings over from these neighbouring areas.
- Across studies, users want three controls: (a) ask follow-up questions, (b) control the level of detail (concise vs. detailed), and (c) get structural or overview information first, such as the chart type or layout, before details. Repeated mentions of verbosity and goal mismatch suggest that a short default with "more detail on request" fits users better than long first answers.
- The 22.2% false-information figure from the diary study, if confirmed, means roughly one in five responses had an error. That is too high to use unverified as the only source of math content for a blind student.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed controlled study was found that evaluates Be My AI, Seeing AI or ChatGPT on **math worksheets or equations** with blind students.
- No peer-reviewed evaluation was found for **Mathpix + screen reader** or **MathCAT** in combination with an AI pipeline. In this session I found no source describing MathCAT, so its details (for example, whether it uses AI or is rule-based MathML-to-speech) must come from another researcher or another source.
- The method behind the diary study's 56.6% and 22.2% figures, and its trust analysis, could not be verified because the full text was not accessible.
- Findings of the "replay" and "user control" features were not found as measured outcomes in any paper.

## 2. What failure modes are documented for VLMs describing math, diagrams and charts for blind users, and what mitigations are recommended?

### Takeaway
Benchmark work shows that VLMs perceive diagrams poorly. They fall back on text cues ("blind faith in text") and fail at basic spatial and geometric perception. BLV-user studies document hallucinated details, misread complex layouts, overlong or off-goal descriptions, and claims the chart does not support. Recommended mitigations: let users query specific details, show variation or uncertainty across several generated descriptions, offer optional confidence information (users disagree on this), support contesting outputs, guide the camera interactively, and give structure first.

### Cited Findings
**Benchmark evidence (not BLV users, but directly relevant to math diagrams)**
- **[Preprint] "Math Blind: Failures in Diagram Understanding Undermine Reasoning in MLLMs."** Weak diagram perception leads models to show "blind faith in text," relying on textual shortcuts instead of visual understanding. The paper introduces the MATHEMETRIC benchmark for diagram perception. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.20745); [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.20745)
- **[Preprint, 2024] "Vision language models are blind."** Four state-of-the-art VLMs performed "shockingly poor[ly]" on simple low-level visual tasks. The authors liken VLM vision to that of a person with significant visual impairment. The paper is from 2024, so newer models may do better. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.06581v1)
- **[Preprint] "Vision-Language Models Can't See the Obvious."** Only the title was visible. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.04741)
- **[Preprint, 2026] "The Aftermath of DrawEduMath: Vision Language Models Underperform with Struggling Students and Misdiagnose Errors."** Per its title, VLMs misdiagnose errors in students' handwritten math work and perform worse with struggling students. Only the title was visible. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.00925)
- **[Grey] Hechinger Report "Researchers combat AI hallucinations in math."** Coverage of math hallucination mitigation. The snippet had no details. — [Hechinger Report](https://hechingerreport.org/proof-points-combat-ai-hallucinations-math/)

**BLV-user evidence**
- **Hallucination rate:** 22.2% of responses contained false information in the CHI '26 diary study (second-hand figure). The study grouped GPT-4o descriptions by number of hallucinations. — [TechXplore](https://techxplore.com/news/2026-04-ai-tools-vision-impaired-good.html); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.13469)
- **Complex layouts misread** and bias against non-English or non-Western materials. — [ACM DL, Misfitting With AI](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3663548.3675659)
- **Unsupported claims in chart descriptions:** "Chart-Supported or Model-Supplied? Examining MLLM-Generated Claims for Accessible Visualization" studies MLLM claims that come from the model rather than the chart, which is a direct framing of hallucinated values. Only the title and reference list were visible. **[Preprint]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.25021)
- **Summarizing instead of reading:** the AFB review of Ray-Ban Meta found the glasses sometimes summarized text instead of reading it in full, or gave less-than-accurate information. **[Grey]** — [AFB AccessWorld](https://afb.org/aw/fall2025/meta-glasses-review)
- **Cognitive load** is higher when interpreting LLM answers about unfamiliar charts. **[Preprint]** — [TU Graz PDF](https://visdesignlab.tugraz.at/papers/2026_vis_tactile-llm.pdf)

**Mitigations recommended in the research**
- **Contestability and interactive camera guidance:** participants wanted more interactive guidance for camera framing, ways to understand image-quality problems, and ways to contest outputs. — [ACM DL, Misfitting With AI](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3663548.3675659)
- **Confidence ratings got mixed reactions:** some users find them useful for quickly dismissing weak results; others say they interrupt their workflow. Confidence statements should therefore be optional or brief, not forced. — [author PDF](https://jayl.in/papers/2024_ASSETS_misfitting.pdf); [Moonlight review](https://www.themoonlight.io/en/review/misfitting-with-ai-how-blind-people-verify-and-contest-ai-errors)
- **Show variation to calibrate trust:** "Surfacing Variations to Calibrate Perceived Reliability of MLLM-generated Image Descriptions" proposes showing users how several generated descriptions differ, so they can judge reliability. Details were not verifiable. **[Preprint, 2025]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.15692)
- **Proactive descriptions based on real BLV questions:** "Guiding Multimodal LLMs with Blind and Low Vision People Visual Questions for Proactive Visual Interpretations" uses BLV people's actual visual questions to steer what the model describes first. **[Preprint]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.01576)
- **Accessibility-aware evaluation:** "Are Large Vision-Language Models Ready to Guide BLV Individuals?" builds an evaluator based on BLV user preferences. This is navigation-focused. **[Preprint]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.00766)
- **Ground answers in extracted data, not pixels:** GraphWhisper uses chart-specific prompting to extract data from the image before answering. VizAbility pairs LLM Q&A with structured keyboard navigation of the underlying chart. — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3772363.3799030); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.09611)

### Inferences
- For math, the most dangerous failure is **silent misreading**: a wrong exponent, sign, fraction bar or subscript, or a wrong spatial relation in a geometry figure. The model then reasons fluently from the wrong premise, which is the "blind faith in text" pattern. A blind user cannot see the source to catch it. Mitigations that follow:
  - convert the image to a structured form (LaTeX/MathML) first, then **read back the extracted problem** for the user to confirm before tutoring starts;
  - when a symbol is ambiguous, flag it explicitly ("I am not sure whether this is x² or x2");
  - let the user query specific elements ("what is in the denominator?").
- Because users split on confidence ratings, a good pattern is to flag uncertainty only on the specific low-confidence elements, instead of putting a global confidence score on every answer.

### Gaps
- No study was found that measures VLM symbol-level error rates on printed or handwritten math **as photographed by blind users**, who often produce blurry, cropped or tilted images.
- The effectiveness of the "surfacing variations" approach could not be confirmed because the full text was not accessible.

## 3. What do studies say about LLM tutors giving away answers vs. Socratic tutoring, and how is hint leakage evaluated?

### Takeaway
Strong evidence, including a peer-reviewed field RCT with about 1,000 Turkish high-school students, shows that unrestricted GPT-4 access improves practice scores but **harms unassisted learning**. A guardrailed "tutor" prompt largely removes that harm, though it does not produce gains on the exam. NLP benchmarks show LLM tutors reveal solutions too early. Leakage is measured with metrics such as Telling@k. Prompt-only guardrails are fragile against adversarial students and against "implicit leakage" through overly detailed hints, so layered defenses are used: a judge model checks replies, and behavioral signals are monitored.

### Cited Findings
- **[PR] Bastani et al., PNAS 2025, "Generative AI without guardrails can harm learning: Evidence from high school mathematics."** A field experiment with about 1,000 high-school math students in Turkey compared "GPT Base," a ChatGPT-like interface, with "GPT Tutor," a version with teacher-informed prompts that gives hints, not answers. — [PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2422633122); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=4895486)
  - On practice problems, GPT Base scored **+48%** and GPT Tutor **+127%** relative to control. — [Wharton Knowledge](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/without-guardrails-generative-ai-can-harm-education/); [Tech & Learning](https://www.techlearning.com/news/high-school-math-students-used-a-gpt-4-ai-tutor-they-did-worse)
  - On the unassisted exam, GPT Base scored **−17%** relative to control. GPT Tutor showed **no significant difference** from control: it avoided the harm but did not improve on control. The authors describe students as using GPT-4 as a "crutch." — [Wharton Knowledge](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/without-guardrails-generative-ai-can-harm-education/)
  - Limitations: one school and one subject. A correction notice was published on August 20, 2025; its content was not checked. — [PNAS correction](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2518204122); [Tech & Learning](https://www.techlearning.com/news/high-school-math-students-used-a-gpt-4-ai-tutor-they-did-worse)
- **[PR] MathDial (EMNLP Findings 2023).** A dataset of about 3,000 teacher–student tutoring dialogues on multi-step math. LLMs failed as tutors because they produced factually incorrect feedback and revealed solutions too early. The Telling@k metric flags a tutor revealing the final answer before the student succeeds within k turns. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.14536v2); [GitHub](https://github.com/eth-nlped/mathdial)
- **[Preprint] MathTutorBench.** Per a secondary summary, models optimized for math problem-solving often have lower pedagogical quality, struggle to scaffold without revealing the answer, and reveal solutions quickly. The primary text was not verified. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/389392159_MathTutorBench_A_Benchmark_for_Measuring_Open-ended_Pedagogical_Capabilities_of_LLM_Tutors)
- **[Preprint] "Unifying AI Tutor Evaluation" (evaluation taxonomy).** In its tutor comparison, GPT-4 revealed the answer too quickly, and Llama-3.1-405B was the most effective tutor. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.09416v1)
- **[Preprint, 2026] "Evaluating Answer Leakage Robustness of LLM Tutors against Adversarial Student Attacks."** A Qwen-7B tutor's leakage rate rose from **14%** against a basic simulated student to **82%** against a fine-tuned adversarial student agent. The paper defines **implicit leakage**: hints so detailed that they effectively give the solution away. Its mitigation is a separate **judge agent** that checks each tutor reply before the student sees it. — [alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2604.18660)
- **[PR, MDPI Applied Sciences 2026] SocraGate.** Inference-time admission control to reduce answer leakage in scaffolded tutoring. It defines leakage as premature disclosure and reports 47 answer-revealing responses in a MathDial audit. — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/16/21/10561)
- **[Preprint, 2026] "LLMs Are Already Good Tutors: Training-Free Prompt Optimization."** Frames the core tension: more explicit information raises immediate solving success but also increases answer leakage. Uses MathTutorBench for out-of-distribution evaluation. — [alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2605.27088)
- **[Preprint, 2026] "Turning Errors into Successes: Designing LLM Tutoring Around Scaffolded Error."** Only the title was visible. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.20061)
- **[Preprint, 2026] "Faster Completion, Less Learning."** GenAI reduced study time on math problems and the knowledge they build. Only the title was visible. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.21629)
- **Khanmigo [Grey/secondary]:** its Socratic behavior comes from system prompts, and it asks guiding questions instead of giving answers. A secondary case-study source reports a 2026 arXiv paper on a 2-year Khanmigo deployment in 18 middle schools, in which students engaged with the tutor in only **17%** of the sessions where they made errors. That is an engagement problem, not a leakage finding; it was not verified against the primary paper. — [buildmvpfast case study](https://www.buildmvpfast.com/blog/ai-tutoring-khanmigo-case-study-2026)
- **[Grey] Developer evidence on detector limits:** an open-source Socratic tutor's leak detector caught direct, equivalent-form and step leaks but missed 19 of 20 disguised leaks, such as "take 10 away from 65/6" or spelling an answer out letter by letter. This is a single developer's test. — [GitHub socratic-tutor-agent](https://github.com/HaiNguyen-python/socratic-tutor-agent)

### Inferences
- For blind students the stakes may be higher: re-reading a problem by ear is costly, so the temptation to ask "just tell me the answer" may be stronger. No source tested this, so it is an inference only. A tutor for this group should combine:
  - a hint ladder, which the PNAS GPT Tutor approximated;
  - an output-side leak check, as in the judge-agent design;
  - logging of leakage, using something like Telling@k, in its own evaluation.
- Implicit leakage is especially relevant in spoken mode. A "step-by-step" spoken hint that says the intermediate result aloud effectively gives the answer.

### Gaps
- No study was found on answer leakage or Socratic tutoring **specifically with BLV learners** or in **voice-only** tutoring.
- The primary Khanmigo efficacy paper and its exact figures were not verified.

## 4. Are there studies on smart glasses (Meta Ray-Ban, Envision Glasses) for reading printed material by blind users, including math?

### Takeaway
There are product announcements, qualitative reviews and early research prototypes, but I found **no peer-reviewed controlled study of Ray-Ban Meta or Envision Glasses for reading printed material, and none at all covering math.** Reading text is the task BLV users most want from smart devices. Reviewers report that Meta AI sometimes summarizes instead of reading verbatim, which is a critical flaw for math.

### Cited Findings
- **[Product] Meta, May 2025 (Global Accessibility Awareness Day).** Meta added a "Detailed responses" accessibility setting so Meta AI on Ray-Ban Meta gives richer descriptions of the scene. Path: Meta AI app → Glasses → Accessibility → Detailed responses. Meta also expanded Be My Eyes "Call a Volunteer" to all 18 Meta AI countries ("Hey Meta, Be My Eyes"). Meta's current page says 21 markets. — [Meta newsroom](https://about.fb.com/news/2025/05/advancing-accessibility-meta/?noamp=mobile); [Meta BLV page](https://www.meta.com/ai-glasses/blind-visually-impaired/); [UploadVR](https://www.uploadvr.com/ray-ban-meta-glasses-meta-ai-detailed-responses-blind-low-vision/); [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/ai/metas-smart-glasses-will-soon-provide-detailed-information-regarding-visual-stimuli-153046605.html)
- **[Grey] AFB AccessWorld review (Fall 2025).** Ray-Ban Meta glasses were not designed for blind or low-vision users but were useful for OCR reading. They **sometimes summarized instead of reading fully**, or gave less-than-accurate information. — [AFB](https://afb.org/aw/fall2025/meta-glasses-review)
- **[Grey] Blind-user accounts and training material:** first-person reviews, an AppleVis forum thread about a "big flaw" with Meta AI and the glasses, and a BVA training guide for BLV users. — [ABILITY Magazine](https://abilitymagazine.com/meta-ray-bans-blind-users-experience-with-smart-glasses/); [AppleVis comment](https://applevis.com/comment/167136); [AppleVis Ray-Ban Display review](https://applevis.com/forum/assistive-technology/meta-ray-ban-display-neural-band-blind-users-honest-take-future-wearable); [BVA guide PDF](https://bva.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Meta-Glasses-Training-Guide-for-Blind-and-Low-Vision-Users.pdf)

### Inferences
- For math, **summarizing instead of reading verbatim** is disqualifying. A design should require a "read exactly, symbol by symbol" mode and a read-back confirmation of the extracted problem before any explanation.
- Wearable cameras make photos easier to take hands-free, but they add framing problems; the "Misfitting" study's call for interactive camera guidance applies.

### Gaps
- No controlled evaluation of Ray-Ban Meta, Envision or any smart glasses for reading **math or STEM printed material** was found.
- No accuracy data was found for Envision's or Meta AI's OCR on equations, fractions, tables or diagrams.

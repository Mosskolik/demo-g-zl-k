## Q1. What do the DIAGRAM Center / WGBH NCAM guidelines say about math (geometry, graphs, tables, number lines, Venn diagrams)?

### Takeaway
DIAGRAM Center and NCAM guidance is consistent. Describe the *parts* (points, lines, labels, values), not the concept. Keep math descriptions brief and specific. Use a linear reading order (left to right, top to bottom) with bullets or line breaks. Do not repeat what the caption or text already says. Convert data graphs (bar, pie, often line) into tables. Leave out purely visual attributes such as color unless the question needs them. Use a layered structure: a brief summary first, then extended description and/or the data.

### Cited Findings
- [GUIDELINE] DIAGRAM Center Mathematics guideline: "Describe the parts of the image rather than explain the concept." It covers graphs, math diagrams, geometry, and equations and expressions. — [DIAGRAM Center, Specific Guidelines – Mathematics](http://diagramcenter.org/specific-guidelines-g.html)
- [GUIDELINE] DIAGRAM Center (Diagrams: Illustrated): the description "should not attempt to teach the concept." When an image has several similar parts, use the same order, sentence structure and words for each part. — [DIAGRAM Center, Specific Guidelines – Diagrams: Illustrated](http://diagramcenter.org/specific-guidelines-c.html)
- [GUIDELINE] Math diagrams: "Traditional descriptions of math diagrams benefit from descriptions that are brief and specific." "Organize the description in a linear fashion, in this case, moving left to right and use bullet points or line breaks to aid in navigation." — [DIAGRAM Center, Mathematics](http://diagramcenter.org/specific-guidelines-g.html); same wording in [NCAM Effective Practices, Example H: Standard Diagrams and Illustrations](https://www.wgbh.org/foundation/services/ncam/tools-resources/effective-practices-for-description-of-science-content-example-h-standard-diagrams-and-illustrations) and the [archived NCAM Example H](http://ncamftp.wgbh.org/ncam-old-site/experience_learn/educational_media/stemdx/exh.html)
- [GUIDELINE] Do not repeat the caption. In the standard math diagram example, the description "should focus on what is not included in the caption, i.e., the points and lines." — [NCAM Example H](https://www.wgbh.org/foundation/services/ncam/tools-resources/effective-practices-for-description-of-science-content-example-h-standard-diagrams-and-illustrations)
- [GUIDELINE] Graphs in the math section: state the type of graph, describe the x- and y-axes and what they represent, then write out the points in bullet form. Number line example: give the endpoints (e.g. "a line running from 0 to 6"), then the equation separately. — [DIAGRAM Center, Mathematics](http://diagramcenter.org/specific-guidelines-g.html)
- [GUIDELINE] Graphs: bar, line and pie graphs are generally best converted to an accessible table with a title and axis labels. If a sentence or two can summarize the graph, that may be enough; otherwise give the data in a table with row and column headings. Bar colors (e.g. dark blue vs. light blue) need not be described unless there is an explicit need, such as a question that refers to the colors. Scatter plots: identify the image as a scatter plot and focus on the overall trend or change in concentration. — [DIAGRAM Center, Specific Guidelines – Graphs](http://diagramcenter.org/specific-guidelines-e.html)
- [GUIDELINE] Venn diagrams: "focus on the data in the Venn diagram, not on its appearance," and "provide the data in brief statements." — [NCAM Effective Practices – Venn Diagrams](https://www.wgbh.org/foundation/services/ncam/tools-resources/effective-practices-for-description-of-science-content-venn-diagrams)
- [GUIDELINE] Bar charts, pie charts and tables are best presented as tables rather than as narrative description. — [NCAM Effective Practices – Pie Charts](https://www.wgbh.org/foundation/services/ncam/tools-resources/effective-practices-for-description-of-science-content-pie-charts); [NCAM – Line Graphs](https://www.wgbh.org/foundation/services/ncam/tools-resources/effective-practices-for-description-of-science-content-line-graphs)
- [GUIDELINE / survey-informed] Short sentences that focus on the data work best. NCAM reports that its survey participants agreed on this, while noting that many wordings are valid. — [NCAM Example H](https://www.wgbh.org/foundation/services/ncam/tools-resources/effective-practices-for-description-of-science-content-example-h-standard-diagrams-and-illustrations)
- [GUIDELINE] Layered structure: "a brief summary followed by extended description and/or specific data." — [NCAM Guidelines for Describing STEM Images (current)](https://www.wgbh.org/ncam-resources/2026-08-25/effective-practices-for-description-of-science-content-guidelines-for-describing-stem-images); [archived version](http://ncamftp.wgbh.org/ncam-old-site/experience_learn/educational_media/stemdx/guidelines.html)
- [GUIDELINE] Math equations: render them in MathML so the reader's reading system can provide its range of speech options. Do not hand-write prose for equations. — [NCAM – Math Equations](https://www.wgbh.org/foundation/services/ncam/tools-resources/effective-practices-for-description-of-science-content-math-equations)
- [GUIDELINE] Static graphs (NCAM Accessible Digital Media Guideline F, Checkpoint F4): the text description should convey (1) the layout of the graph, (2) the location of variables on the graph, and (3) the overall trend. The worked example names the chart type, then each axis with its variable and range (e.g. x axis temperature, y axis pressure from 0 to 800 mm), then sample points along the curve. — [NCAM Guideline F: Graphs (archived)](http://ncamftp.wgbh.org/ncam-old-site/invent_build/web_multimedia/accessible-digital-media-guide/guideline-f-graphs.html)
- [GUIDELINE] DIAGRAM Center guidelines have two parts: general guidelines (style, language, formatting) and type-specific guidelines. — [DIAGRAM Center General Guidelines](http://diagramcenter.org/general-guidelines-final-draft.html); summarized by [ADCET](https://www.adcet.edu.au/resource/9787/image-description-guidelines)

### Inferences
- Rule set to carry into the app: (1) one-line identification of the figure type, (2) linear order from left to right and top to bottom, (3) one fact per short sentence or bullet, (4) name the parts (points, segments, labels, given values), never the concept or property being tested, (5) no colors or styling unless the question refers to them, (6) skip anything the question text already says, (7) for data graphics, give a table or ordered list instead of prose, (8) offer a summary first and details on request.
- "Overall trend" (NCAM F4, scatter-plot guidance) applies to textbook and instructional description. In an assessment it can conflict with "do not give away the answer" when the question asks about the trend (see Q4).

### Gaps
- Could not read the DIAGRAM Center General Guidelines text verbatim (host blocked), so specific general rules (e.g. on tense, units, or reading symbols) are unconfirmed.
- No DIAGRAM Center or NCAM page found that gives a dedicated geometry procedure (vertex order, tick marks, angle arcs). The math page is reported to cover "geometry," but its rules were not visible in the snippets.

## Q2. What do BANA's Guidelines and Standards for Tactile Graphics (and related braille guidance) say about describing math images?

### Takeaway
BANA treats the image description (and transcriber's note) as a required companion to tactile graphics. A description is required when a tactile graphic is not recommended. It should be concise without being wordy, use vocabulary suited to the reader, and come *before* the graphic. BANA also asks whether a description would reveal data or details the reader is supposed to retrieve or observe, which supports the anti-giveaway principle. Math-specific detail lives in separate BANA guidance on mathematical diagrams, which I could not retrieve.

### Cited Findings
- [GUIDELINE] A recommended tactile graphic should come with an image description. Where a tactile graphic is *not* recommended, a description is *required*. — [CAST AEM, Recommendation 1e: Provide image descriptions for all tactile graphics (BANA-based)](https://aem.cast.org/binaries/content/assets/common/publications/aem/qa-1e-braille-recommendations-2023071432.pdf)
- [GUIDELINE] The transcriber's note and image description are placed before the graphic and can say how many pages the graphic spans. The note, the image description and the original text should be concise without being wordy, while letting the tactile graphic stand on its own. Vocabulary should suit the reader. — [BANA Guidelines and Standards for Tactile Graphics, 2022 (accessible PDF)](https://www.brailleauthority.org/sites/default/files/tg/Tactile%20Graphics%20Standards%20and%20Guidelines%202022_a11y.pdf); [BANA TG web manual](https://www.brailleauthority.org/tg/web-manual/tgmanual.html)
- [GUIDELINE] BANA's decision process (around §7.2.4, per search summary) asks whether the reader would otherwise have to retrieve data or observe details that a description would reveal. In other words, if the task is for the student to extract the information, the description should not pre-extract it. — [BANA TG web manual](https://www.brailleauthority.org/tg/web-manual/tgmanual.html) (*section number from search summary; verify*)
- [GUIDELINE] Notes should match the student's reading level. — [TSBVI, Element Arrangement on a Tactile Graphic Document](https://www.tsbvi.edu/tx-senseabilities/issues/tx-senseabilities-spring-2015/element-arrangement-on-a-tactile-graphic-document)
- [GUIDELINE] Tactile math representations should follow BANA's "Guidelines for Mathematical Diagrams." Nemeth Code governs math braille. — [Teaching Visually Impaired, Tactile graphics guidelines](https://www.teachingvisuallyimpaired.com/tactile-graphics-guidelines.html); [Wikipedia, BANA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braille_Authority_of_North_America)
- [GUIDELINE] Tactile editing: make sure identifiers are unambiguous and point at one item only. — [PIAF, "On Editing Graphics for the Blind" manual](https://piaf-tactile.com/docs/Tactile_Graphics_Manual.pdf)

### Inferences
- For spoken description, carry over: (a) say the description before the student explores or answers, (b) keep it concise but complete enough to stand alone, (c) match the vocabulary to grade level, (d) give each label exactly one referent (e.g. "point A" always means one vertex), (e) do not do the data retrieval the item is testing.

### Gaps
- BANA's math-diagram-specific rules (e.g. how to voice tick marks, angle marks and "not drawn to scale") were not retrieved.
- Older editions (2010, 2012) also circulate, e.g. [PRCVI copy of the 2010 edition](https://www.prcvi.org/media/1125/guidelines-and-standards-for-tactile-graphics.pdf). The 2022 edition is current.

## Q3. The NSF-funded study of expert geometry-diagram descriptions (par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10348278), plus related empirical work

### Takeaway
**I could not retrieve or identify the par.nsf.gov 10348278 study.** WebFetch to par.nsf.gov was blocked, and three differently worded searches did not surface it. Its findings (word counts with vs. without visual access, spatial information used, the "double-edged" effect) are therefore **unverified and must not be reported from these notes**. Related empirical work found: (a) more description improves blind students' answers, but accuracy stays low and some image types resist description (Emerson & Anderson 2018); (b) teachers of students with visual impairments vary widely in how they describe math graphics (Rosenblum et al. 2020); (c) tables, line graphs and item-specific images carry a lot of math content and are hard to describe succinctly.

### Cited Findings
- [EMPIRICAL] Emerson & Anderson (2018), *JVIB*. 44 students with visual impairments listened to math textbook pages under four description conditions with increasing amounts of description, then answered content questions. More description led to better answers, but the best correct rate was low (29%). Correct rates for equations, pie charts and maps were inconsistent or below 15%. The authors conclude that description can convey important math information, but for some images no level of description is sufficient. They recommend providing materials in several formats at once. — [ERIC EJ1175990 record](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1175990); [full text PDF](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1175990.pdf); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/324548261_Using_Description_to_Convey_Mathematics_Content_in_Visual_Images_to_Students_who_Are_Visually_Impaired)
- [EMPIRICAL] Companion textbook-image analysis (Emerson & Anderson 2018): tables, line graphs, and images tied to a single question were frequent, contained a lot of math content and were hard to describe succinctly. Models, line diagrams, calculator keys and number lines lend themselves easily to description. — [ERIC EJ1171951 PDF](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1171951.pdf); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/323277447_What_Mathematical_Images_Are_in_a_Typical_Mathematics_Textbook_Implications_for_Students_with_Visual_Impairments)
- [EMPIRICAL] Rosenblum, Cheng, Zebehazy, Emerson & Beal (2020), *JVIB* 114(3), 231–236. 10 teachers of students with visual impairments described math graphics for two hypothetical students (a low-vision print reader and a braille reader). The main finding was wide variability in how teachers described the graphics and in how they questioned students about graphic features and information. The study also examined consistency with established (NCAM-type) guidelines. Exact compliance figures were not retrieved. — [ERIC EJ1255993](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1255993); [full text PDF](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1255993.pdf); [SAGE abstract](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/0145482X20923442)
- [EMPIRICAL] Students with visual impairments in grades 4–10 needed significantly more help from their teachers when solving math word problems that involved graphics such as diagrams and geometric figures. — [Rosenblum, Cheng & Beal 2018, ERIC EJ1192227](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1192227.pdf)
- [EMPIRICAL/design] Geometry diagrams visually encode properties that the accompanying text does not state, such as whether a point lies on a line. A verbal channel must therefore state these incidence relations explicitly. (System study with 20 sighted and 9 visually impaired participants.) — [Frontiers in Education 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2023.1071759/xml)
- [EMPIRICAL/design] A description is read one-dimensionally and in sequence, while a diagram's spatial layout carries implicit meaning (grouping, proximity) that is lost unless stated. — [Springer, Pattern Collection for Accessible Teaching Materials](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-032-19157-1_12)
- [EMPIRICAL] Audio-tactile researchers explicitly vary description detail because of cognitive load from the growing number of terms in audio description. — [ScienceDirect, audio-tactile presentation of math graphics, IJHCS 2023](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1071581923001313)
- [EMPIRICAL] In a study of diagram access, 14 of 15 blind and low-vision participants relied on sighted helpers, and that workaround had its own problems. — [TADA, arXiv 2311.04502](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.04502)

### Inferences
- Together, Emerson & Anderson and Rosenblum et al. support: (1) a standard, consistent description template (to reduce teacher-style variability), (2) more description being better up to a point, (3) offering alternatives for image types that resist description, such as an explicit data table or a "repeat or more detail" option.
- Incidence relations (point on line, segments meeting at a vertex, parallel or perpendicular markings) should be stated explicitly, because a sighted reader picks them up for free and a listener does not.

- **par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10348278: not retrieved, not identified.** Word counts with vs. without visual access, the spatial-information categories coded, and the "double-edged" effect are all unknown from my research. The report writer should not cite any numbers for this study unless another researcher retrieved it. To retrieve it, allow `par.nsf.gov` in the session network settings.
- Emerson & Anderson's per-condition word counts and the exact description conditions were not visible.

## Q4. How should coordinate graphs and tables be described, and what do assessment programs say about not giving away answers?

### Takeaway
Ordering consensus: name the graph type → title → axes (label, variable, range, scale or interval) → data (points in order, or a table) → trend only where interpretation is not the construct being tested. For assessments, NCAM and NWEA add: describe all answer-choice images in parallel (same length, number of adjectives and structure) so that wording differences do not cue the answer. Stay literal, using the item's own terms (e.g. "blank," "missing symbol"). Assume familiarity with basic shapes and do not define them. Omit visual-only details unless the item needs them. Read-aloud rules (Smarter Balanced) restrict readers to the title or caption and any text in the graphic, and forbid inventing content or going beyond the allowed content.

### Cited Findings
- [GUIDELINE] NWEA, coordinate planes: tell the student the figure is on a coordinate plane. For students new to coordinate planes, add a brief orientation to the grid. Identify the axis labels with their number ranges where applicable. Adjust wording by grade: lower grades may hear "bottom" and "side," grades 4 and up hear "horizontal axis" and "vertical axis." Read coordinates consistently, e.g. "(3, –7)" as "the point 3 comma negative 7." One example warns that a description can become too long and complicated with too many numbers. — [NWEA Image Description Guidelines for Assessments (2021)](https://www.nwea.org/uploads/2022/11/Image-Description-Guidelines-for-Assessments_NWEA_2021.pdf); [Boise State copy](https://boisestate.pressbooks.pub/app/uploads/sites/380/2025/02/Image-Description-Guidelines-for-Assessments_IDGA_NWEA1.pdf)
- [GUIDELINE] NWEA geometry pattern: short alt text ("A diagram.") followed by a longer description that states the given measurements (e.g. a triangle with a base of 12.5 cm). When a diagram is marked "not drawn to scale," state this, because the student must rely on the stated measurements rather than the appearance. — [NWEA guidelines, via search summary](https://www.nwea.org/uploads/2022/11/Image-Description-Guidelines-for-Assessments_NWEA_2021.pdf)
- [GUIDELINE] NCAM assessments: "All images in a test item, including answer choices, should be described in as much the same style as possible. This includes using similar types and number of adjectives and comparable length of descriptions to avoid unintentionally signaling differences between the images." — [NCAM Describing Images for Enhanced Assessments – Math Images (archived)](http://ncamftp.wgbh.org/ncam-old-site/experience_learn/educational_media/describing-images-for-enhanced/math-images.html); [NCAM PD – Math Images](https://www.wgbh.org/foundation/services/ncam/tools-resources/describing-images-for-enhanced%20-assessments-professional-development-math-images)
- [GUIDELINE] NCAM assessments: be literal and use the item's own terms. Because the item refers to a "missing symbol," the box is called "a blank." For fraction-model answer choices, describe the elements (e.g. parts shaded) rather than naming the fraction with "proper terms," which would give the answer. — [NCAM Math Images (archived)](http://ncamftp.wgbh.org/ncam-old-site/experience_learn/educational_media/describing-images-for-enhanced/math-images.html)
- [GUIDELINE] NCAM assessments: assume familiarity with common geometric shapes and concepts (triangles, cylinders, hexagons). Do not define a hexagon, especially when the item tests something else, such as perimeter. — [NCAM Math Images (archived)](http://ncamftp.wgbh.org/ncam-old-site/experience_learn/educational_media/describing-images-for-enhanced/math-images.html)
- [GUIDELINE] NCAM assessments: unless necessary to answer the question, leave out purely visual information such as bar colors. — [NCAM Introduction to Image Description (archived)](http://ncamftp.wgbh.org/ncam-old-site/experience_learn/educational_media/describing-images-for-enhanced/introduction-to-image-descript.html); [current version](https://www.wgbh.org/foundation/services/ncam/tools-resources/describing-images-for-enhanced-assessments-introduction-to-image-description)
- [GUIDELINE] NCAM number lines (assessment example): list the labeled values and the tick spacing, and place the dot relative to the labels, without interpreting what the dot means. — [NCAM Math Images (archived)](http://ncamftp.wgbh.org/ncam-old-site/experience_learn/educational_media/describing-images-for-enhanced/math-images.html)
- [GUIDELINE] NCAM: build image descriptions into item creation, not as a "bolt-on" accommodation after items are final. — [NCAM Guidelines for Describing Images for Assessments (current)](https://www.wgbh.org/ncam-resources/2026-08-07/describing-images-for-enhanced-assessments-guidelines-for-describing-images-for-assessments)
- [GUIDELINE] Smarter Balanced Read Aloud Guidelines: read the title or caption if available. Any text in the body of an image, graphic or diagram may be read, in an order that suits the student. Use grade-appropriate language. First check whether image details are needed before describing them. Do not create sentences when information is missing. Reading beyond the allowed content violates test security. — [Smarter Balanced Read Aloud Guidelines (Cambium-hosted)](https://ct.portal.cambiumast.com/content/contentresources/en/Smarter-Balanced-Read-Aloud-Guidelines.pdf); [Read Aloud Guidelines in Spanish](https://portal.smarterbalanced.org/hubfs/read-aloud-guidelines-in-spanish.pdf); [Simplified Test Directions guidance](https://portal.smarterbalanced.org/hubfs/44715956/guidelines-for-simplified-test-directions-in-the-test-administration-manual.pdf); [Usability, Accessibility and Accommodations Guidelines](https://portal.smarterbalanced.org/hubfs/usability-accessibility-and-accommodations-guidelines.pdf)
- [GUIDELINE] UK: modified large print papers have more white space and diagrams modified for accessibility. Braille papers come with tactile diagrams (with print labels as an option). Pictures may be replaced or supplemented with a written description. — [RNIB, Access to exams and tests](https://www.rnib.org.uk/professionals/education-professionals/access-to-exams-and-tests/); [VIEW, Arrangements for vision-impaired candidates](https://viewweb.org.uk/exam_access/activities/ex_2_2.html); STA MLP/braille mark-scheme amendments, e.g. [KS1 2024 reading](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6655b6168f90ef31c23ebb10/STA248861e_2024_ks1_English_reading_Mark_scheme_amendments_MLP_and_braille.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Do-not-give-away rules for the app:** (1) describe what is drawn (labels, given numbers, marks), never derived properties (do not say "isosceles," "parallel," "the larger angle" or "increasing" if the question asks about that), (2) use the question's own vocabulary, (3) describe all options in parallel, (4) describe models such as shaded fractions by their elements and do not name the answer value, (5) say "not drawn to scale" when the figure says so, (6) give the trend summary only when the trend is not what the question asks for.
- Coordinate-graph template: "A coordinate plane. Horizontal axis [label], from [min] to [max], marked every [step]. Vertical axis [label], from [min] to [max], every [step]. Points/line: [ordered list from left to right]." Tables: "A table with [n] rows and [m] columns. Column headings: … ." Then read row by row, repeating the header with each value.

### Gaps
- I found no PARCC or NAEP-specific diagram-description language, and no explicit STA "reader must not explain the diagram" wording, in search results.
- The exact Smarter Balanced Appendix D wording on graphics (2020) was not verified.

## Q5. Orientation language: clock face, left/right/top/bottom, reference points, angles and marks (right-angle marks, tick marks)

### Takeaway
Little guidance specifically covers *spoken* treatment of tick marks or right-angle symbols. The adjacent guidance converges on: a consistent scanning order (top to bottom, left to right, or clockwise from a fixed reference point / "home base"), describing the components (shapes, lines, labels, symbols) before exploration, unambiguous labels, and grade-appropriate positional words (NWEA: "bottom/side" for young students, "horizontal/vertical axis" from grade 4). Clock-face positions are not mentioned in any math-description guideline I found.

### Cited Findings
- [GUIDELINE] Teach a consistent, organized exploration pattern (top to bottom, left to right, clockwise around a diagram) starting from a reference point or home base, because systematic scanning prevents missed details. Describe components (shapes, lines, textures, labels) before the student explores. — [Teaching Visually Impaired, Tactile graphics instruction](https://www.teachingvisuallyimpaired.com/tactile-graphics-instruction.html); [Perkins, Tips and Strategies to Make Geometry Accessible](https://www.perkins.org/resource/tips-and-strategies-make-geometry-accessible/)
- [GUIDELINE] Linear left-to-right order with line breaks for math diagrams. — [DIAGRAM Center, Mathematics](http://diagramcenter.org/specific-guidelines-g.html)
- [GUIDELINE] Grade-dependent positional vocabulary ("bottom," "side" → "horizontal axis," "vertical axis"). — [NWEA guidelines](https://www.nwea.org/uploads/2022/11/Image-Description-Guidelines-for-Assessments_NWEA_2021.pdf)
- [GUIDELINE] Use clear, uncrowded figures and let the learner inspect the whole shape before answering. — [Braille Doodle, Accessible STEM activities](https://www.brailledoodle.org/accessible-stem-tactile-graphs-maps-activities/)
- [GUIDELINE] Identifiers should be unambiguous and point at one item. — [PIAF manual](https://piaf-tactile.com/docs/Tactile_Graphics_Manual.pdf)

### Inferences
- Proposed geometry speaking order (synthesized, **not** from a single source): (1) figure type and count ("a triangle ABC," "a quadrilateral"), (2) reference/start point ("Point A is at the top"), (3) vertices in a fixed direction, clockwise or the labelled order, with position words such as top, bottom left, bottom right, (4) each side with its given length ("side AB is labelled 5 cm"), (5) marks stated literally, not interpreted: "there is a right-angle mark at B," "sides AB and AC each carry one tick mark" (rather than "AB equals AC"? see note), (6) interior points, lines, extensions and incidences ("point D lies on side BC"), (7) unknowns ("angle C is marked x"), (8) "not drawn to scale" if shown.
- Note on tick marks: saying "one tick mark on each of AB and AC" stays literal. Saying "AB and AC are equal" interprets the symbol. When the item tests knowledge of what tick marks mean, the literal form avoids giving away the answer. When it does not, the interpreted form may be clearer. This is an inference from NCAM's "describe elements, not proper terms" principle, not a stated rule.
- Clock-face orientation (e.g. "point C is at 3 o'clock") is common in orientation and mobility practice, but I found no math-description guideline that endorses it. Treat it as optional.

### Gaps
- No source found that prescribes how to voice right-angle squares, angle arcs, arrowheads for parallel lines, or tick marks in spoken description.
- No empirical comparison found of clock-face vs. left/right/top/bottom language for geometry.
- The NSF expert-description study (Q3) presumably addresses spatial language, but it was not retrieved.

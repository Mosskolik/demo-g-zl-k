## Q1. UK: JCQ AARA (reader section, decoding, memory aid) and STA KS2

### Takeaway
- **JCQ (GCSE/A-level):** the reader "must only read the instructions of the question paper and questions, and must not explain or clarify". They repeat text only when the candidate shows a specific need. A vision-impaired candidate can get help retrieving information from diagrams, but only on request, and only the same visual information a sighted candidate has. Approved candidates may have symbols **decoded**, which means named, never explained.
- **STA (KS2 SATs):** stricter. The reader "may read words and numbers, but not mathematical symbols". They read subject vocabulary but do not clarify it. They may point to diagrams but "must not explain the information or help the pupil by interpreting it".

### Cited Findings
**JCQ AARA 2024–25, section 5.5 "Computer reader/reader" (pp. 53–58) [TAM METİN]**: [Pearson-hosted JCQ AARA 2024-25](https://qualifications.pearson.com/content/dam/pdf/Support/Access-arrangements/jcq-aara-accessible.pdf). Official URL, which returned 403: [JCQ](https://www.jcq.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/JCQ-AARA-24-25_FINAL_accessible.pdf).

- **5.5.2 (computer reader):** "Computer software which accurately reads out text … but does not decode or interpret the paper, may be used as computer reader." This is the official precedent for a machine reader. It may read; it may not decode or interpret.
- **5.5.3 (what a reader is):** "A reader is a responsible adult who reads the instructions of the question paper and the questions to the candidate. This may involve reading the whole paper to the candidate or the candidate may request only some words to be read."
- **5.5.4 (what a reader is not):** "A reader is not a Communication Professional, a practical assistant, a prompter or a scribe."
- **5.5.8 (diagrams):** "A reader would bring their own interpretation to a description of a diagram. This could affect a candidate's response and compromise the reliability of the qualification. A reader should only help a vision impaired candidate retrieve information found in diagrams, graphs, maps and tables in response to a request from the candidate."
- **"The rules – the use of a reader".** This is the full list, in the order printed:
  - **ALLOWED:** "may help a vision impaired candidate retrieve information found in diagrams, graphs, maps and tables so that he/she has access to the same visual information as a sighted candidate. The candidate must make clear what help he/she requires;"
  - **ALLOWED:** "may read numbers printed in figures as words (e.g. 356 would be read as three hundred and fifty six, but when reading the number it should also be pointed to on the question paper). An exception would be when the question is asking for a number to be written in words …;"
  - **ALLOWED:** "may decode symbols and unit abbreviations in Maths and Science examinations for candidates who require this arrangement to access the assessment, reflecting the candidate's current and normal way of working within the centre;"
  - **ALLOWED:** "may, if requested, give the spelling of a word which appears on the paper but otherwise spellings must not be given;"
  - **ALLOWED:** "may read back, when requested, what the candidate has written without any emphasis on any errors;"
  - **REQUIRED:** "must read accurately;"
  - **REQUIRED:** "must have appropriate subject knowledge in order to act effectively as a reader in Maths and Science examinations, and decode symbols and formulae with accuracy;"
  - **FORBIDDEN:** "**must only read the instructions of the question paper and questions, and must not explain or clarify;**"
  - **FORBIDDEN:** "must only repeat the instructions of the question paper or questions when a candidate indicates a specific need for help;"
  - **FORBIDDEN:** "must not advise the candidate regarding which questions to do, when to move on to the next question, nor the order in which questions should be answered;"
  - **FORBIDDEN:** "must not decode any symbols in a Music examination."
  - The rule that failing to follow these "could lead to the disqualification of the candidate".
- **Worked examples (p. 57–58):**
  - Explanation request: "She asks the reader to explain. The reader reads the question again but cannot offer any explanation. A reader can only read the text and not explain it."
  - Shape request (directly relevant to math): "A candidate with vision impairment asks the reader if an unlabelled shape on a diagram has five sides and is a pentagon. The reader can state how many sides the shape has but cannot confirm that it is a pentagon. A reader can only help retrieve visual information that a sighted candidate has access to."
  - Blind A-level Maths candidate: "The reader will enable the candidate to identify diagrams correctly."
- **Appendix 1, "Memory aid for a reader" (p. 121) [TAM METİN]:** "I am here to read for you in your exam. You must make clear what you want to be read." / "I can only read the instructions and the questions." / "I can repeat instructions, but only if you make it clear which instructions you want me to read." / "I can't tell you which questions to choose. I can't tell you when to move on to the next question. I can't tell you which questions to do first." / "I can spell words if you ask me, but only words on the question paper." / "I can read back your answer, but only if you ask me." The appendix also requires that "The candidate must be made aware of what a reader can and cannot do in advance of their first examination". Source: [Pearson-hosted JCQ AARA 2024-25](https://qualifications.pearson.com/content/dam/pdf/Support/Access-arrangements/jcq-aara-accessible.pdf).
- **JCQ Notice to Centres on decoding (©JCQ 2019, effective 28 Oct 2019) [TAM METİN]:**
  - Who it is for: "From 2019/20 readers can now decode symbols and unit abbreviations in Mathematics and Science." It is intended for "a candidate who is unable to independently access any of the text and symbols … such as a candidate with a significant visual impairment who cannot read Braille, cannot access tactile diagrams…". For most other candidates, "the reader would point to the symbol."
  - Definition: "**The term 'decoding' means the naming of the symbol. It does not involve explaining when or how the symbol is to be used or describing the symbol.**"
  - Examples: 2² → "two squared"; ≤ → "less than or equal to"; ∈ → "is an element of"; ∞ → "infinity"; ∛ → "cube root"; θ → "Theta"; ∫ → "Integral"; CO2 → "the reader reads each letter/number".
  - Source: [Decoding guidance for readers (Pearson copy)](https://qualifications.pearson.com/content/dam/pdf/Support/Access-arrangements/Decoding-guidance-for-readers.pdf)
- **Older JCQ "Regulations – Reader" memory aid (2016, superseded on decoding) [TAM METİN]:**
  - Diagrams: the reader "may enable a vision impaired candidate to identify diagrams, graphs and tables but must not give factual information nor offer any suggestions, other than that information which would be available on the paper for sighted candidates".
  - Symbols: the reader "must not decode any symbols and unit abbreviations, e.g. 2² should not be read as two squared, but the function simply pointed to by the reader. Part of the assessment is recognising what the superscript 2 means. Similarly, if the symbol > is printed, it should not be read as 'greater than' but simply pointed to".
  - Source: [JCQ memory aid 2016](https://learningsupportcentre.com/content/uploads/2024/10/JCQ-Reg-Memory-Aid-Reader-and-Scribe-2016.pdf)

**STA, 2027 Key Stage 2 access arrangements guidance (GOV.UK, updated 11 November 2026), "Readers" [TAM METİN]**: [GOV.UK 2027 KS2 AA guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/key-stage-2-tests-access-arrangements/2027-key-stage-2-access-arrangements-guidance--2)

- Readers "do not need to be specialists in the subject being tested", but they "must know what may or may not be read to a pupil in particular tests" and "must understand any subject-specific issues that may occur".
- **Mathematics:** "A reader may help a pupil to read the mathematics tests by: clarifying instructions, provided that no additional information is given which could give the pupil an advantage; **reading, but not clarifying, subject-specific vocabulary**. **STA, Key Stage 2 test administration guidance (2026 tests; GOV.UK, updated 30 April 2026), Mathematics Papers 1–3 "Assistance" [TAM METİN]**: [GOV.UK KS2 TAG](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/key-stage-2-tests-test-administration-guidance-tag/key-stage-2-test-administration-guidance)
- Reading on request: "If a pupil requests it, you may read a question to them on a one-to-one basis. If reading to a pupil, you can read words and numbers but not mathematical symbols. This is to ensure pupils are not given an advantage by having the function inadvertently explained by reading its name."
- Paper 1: "You must ensure you do not convey information that would give pupils an advantage."
- Papers 2–3: "At a pupil's request, you may point to parts of the test paper such as charts, diagrams, statements and equations, but **you must not explain the information or help the pupil by interpreting it. You must not explain any subject-specific terminology.** If any context or words related to a question are unfamiliar to a pupil, you may provide explanations or show the pupil related objects or pictures…"
- Scripted refusals, which can serve as a voice-assistant template:
  - Paper 1: Q "Do I need to multiply when I calculate 95% of 240?" → A "I cannot tell you, but think hard and try to remember. We can talk about it after the test."
  - Papers 2–3: Q "What does 'quadrilateral' or '>' or '<' mean?" → A "I cannot tell you, but think hard and try to remember. We can talk about it after the test."
  - Papers 2–3: Q "What is '0.6'?" → A "That is nought point six." Reading a number is allowed.

### Inferences
- JCQ and STA draw the same line in different places. JCQ lets an approved candidate hear a symbol's **name** ("less than or equal to") and never its **use**. STA bans even the name, because the name itself can explain the function.
- Both distinguish **non-mathematical context words**, which STA lets the reader explain, from **mathematical terminology**, which must never be explained. A voice assistant should apply the same split: it may explain everyday context words (a name, an object), but never math terms or symbols.
- JCQ's pentagon example gives an operational test: the assistant may report what is **perceptible** (count of sides, labels, values) but not what is **inferred** (the shape's name, a property, a trend).
- JCQ requires the candidate to know in advance what the reader can and cannot do. That justifies an up-front "here is what I can and can't do" script in the assistant.

- JCQ AARA **2025–26** (valid 1 Sep 2025–31 Aug 2026, updated March 2026) could not be read because jcq.org.uk returns 403. Wording is verified only for 2024–25. Whether 5.5 changed in 2025–26 or in the March 2026 update is unverified.
- The STA "modified test pack" guidance for visually impaired pupils (mentioned in the TAG) is not public online and was not read.

## Q2. USA: Smarter Balanced, PARCC/New Meridian, NAEP, and state guidelines (Texas, Pennsylvania, Louisiana, South Dakota, Rhode Island)

### Takeaway
US programs share one rule set: read verbatim, in a neutral tone, emphasizing only what the print emphasizes. They "do not paraphrase, interpret, define, or translate", and they answer student questions only by repeating the text. They differ on math notation:
- **Negative vs minus:** negative numbers are "negative", subtraction is "minus" (universal).
- **Fractions:** Smarter Balanced reads common fractions as words ("two thirds"); Pennsylvania uses "the fraction three over four".
- **Inequality symbols:** Texas says "symbol", Pennsylvania grades 3–5 say "a symbol", Smarter Balanced and Pennsylvania grades 6+ say "less than".
- **Graphs and tables:** read in a fixed order (title, headings or axes, values) with no interpretation.

### Cited Findings
**Smarter Balanced Read Aloud Guidelines (Connecticut/Cambium edition, PDF created Sept 2026) [TAM METİN]**: [CT Cambium Smarter Balanced Read Aloud Guidelines](https://ct.portal.cambiumast.com/content/contentresources/en/Smarter-Balanced-Read-Aloud-Guidelines.pdf)

Any text that appears in the body of the image/graphic/diagram may be read … Often the reader moves top to bottom, left to right in a clockwise direction, or general to specific".

- **Math reading tables (Tables 1–5):**
  - Decimals: 0.056 → "zero point zero five six".
  - Common fractions: "one half, one fourth, two thirds, four fifths".
  - Less common fractions are read as "numerator over denominator": 14/25 → "fourteen over twenty-five".
  - Mixed numbers: 3½ → "three and one half".
  - **"Negative numbers - do NOT read negative sign as 'minus'"**: −3 → "negative three", −5/8 → "negative five eighths".
  - Ratio x:y → "x to y"; √6 → "the square root of six".
  - Subtraction: 487 − 159 → "four hundred eighty-seven minus one hundred fifty-nine equals"; 13 + 27 = ? → "… equals question mark".
  - Box: 3+□=8 → "three plus box equals eight".
  - Variables: 8x − 3 → "eight 'x' minus three".
  - Parentheses: 4(y − 2) + 5 = 7 → "four open parenthesis 'y' minus two close parenthesis plus five equals seven".
  - Inequality: |t| − 2 over 6 ≤ 15 → "the absolute value of 't' (pause) minus two (pause) over six is less than or equal to fifteen".
  - Functions: f(x) → "F of x"; f(g(x)) → "F of g of x".
  - Points: (−1, 2) → "the point (pause) negative one comma two".
  - Geometry: AB ∥ CD → "line segment AB is parallel to line segment CD"; ∠ABC ≅ ∠DEF → "angle A B C is congruent to angle D E F".
  - Trigonometry: sin25° → "sine twenty-five degrees".

Worked example: "The title of the table is Results from School Walk-a-Thon. The table has 2 columns and 4 rows. From left to right, the column headings read … the first row reads thirty, one hundred twelve. The second row reads …"
- **Suggested human reader script, for the student before test day:**
  - "I cannot help you with any answers."
  - "I will be using a very direct voice that does not change very much…"
  - "**Sometimes there may be something about a word or phrase that might give you a hint if I read it out loud. In those cases, I will skip the word, point to it on screen …, and continue to read.**"
  - "You can ask me to re-read parts of the test…"
  - "**If you ask me a question about the test all I will say is: 'Do your best work. I cannot help you with that.'**"

### Inferences
- Rules common to all US programs:
  - **Allowed:** verbatim reading; repeating on request; spelling words; changing speed or volume; stating that a word is printed in bold, italics or capitals; describing a visual in a fixed order (title, labels or axes, values).
  - **Forbidden:** paraphrasing, interpreting, defining, translating; conversing about the item; emphasis not in print; inflection or pauses that cue; solving while reading; reading a part that would itself cue the answer.
- The distinction between directions and items is consistent. NAEP and STA allow directions to be clarified or reworded. **Items** never may be. A voice assistant can therefore rephrase *how to answer* ("say the letter of your choice") but never *what the question means*.
- Naming inequality symbols is the most contested point: STA says "not at all", Texas and PA grades 3–5 say "symbol", JCQ (approved candidates), Smarter Balanced and PA grades 6+ say "less than". When knowing the symbol is the construct (e.g., "which symbol makes this true?"), the conservative choice is "a symbol" or skip-and-point (Smarter Balanced script).
- The Texas rule "x equals the values shown" and the Pennsylvania tally rule ("some tally marks are shown") show that **reading can itself be the answer**. A machine reader needs item-level flags for "do not verbalize".

## Q3. College Board (SAT/AP), ACT, ETS

### Takeaway
ETS has the most detailed private-testing reader protocol. It says read verbatim, don't try to solve, keep a neutral face, avoid conversation, and repeat on request. It also has its own math-reading rules. College Board and ACT publicly confirm only that a human reader exists. College Board says it supplies its own reader script, which is not public.

### Cited Findings
**ETS, "Guidelines for a Test Reader" (© 2002) [TAM METİN]**: [ETS PDF](https://www.eu.ets.org/pdfs/disabilities/guidelines-for-test-reader.pdf)
- Characteristic 3: "Ability to follow instructions to read, verbatim, only the words in the test book or on the screen, without changing or adding words or assisting the test-taker in selecting a response."
- General 5: "Your task is to read only the test questions. Do not try to solve problems or determine the correct answer as you read because this may result in an unconscious pause or change in inflection that could be misleading … The expression on your face should remain neutral."
- General 6: "Do not give your own emphasis to words not emphasized in print."
- General 10: "Avoid getting into conversation about the test questions, but try to respond to the test taker's questions by repeating the item, words, or instructions as needed."
- Multiple-choice rule 1: "equally stress each response option". Rule 4: "Do you want me to read the question again ... or any part of it?"
- **Mathematics reading:**
  - Note-taking on dictation: "A test taker is permitted to ask the reader to write notes and to assist with intermediate steps in computing mathematics problems … The test taker should be specific in directions to the reader as to what he or she writes". The reader works as a dictated scratchpad and does no computing of their own.
  - General rule: "Use technically-correct yet simple terms, and be consistent in the treatment of similar expressions."
  - (a) xy → "x y" (or "x times y" if unclear).
  - (b) Case: "Capital and lower-case letters should be differentiated": "Capital R minus two y equals six".
  - (c) Fractions: simple fractions are read as fractions ("five sixths"); letter fractions as "a over b".
  - (d) Complex fractions: "a fraction with numerator b plus d and denominator c" … "If there is any question as to where the fraction ends, say 'end fraction.'"
  - (e) "Negative numbers should be read as 'negative.' e.g., -5 should be read as 'negative five,' not 'minus five.' When a subtraction operation is involved, read the sign as 'minus'".
  - (f) Parentheses may be "quantity … end quantity", "paren, close paren" or "left paren, right paren". Example: "Use pauses to audibly group sections of an expression together."
  - (g) Numbered equations with a printed legend: read the number, "Give the meaning of each letter or symbol" (from the printed legend), then the equation.
- Test center: "Discussion or communication concerning interpretation of test content is not permitted … ETS reserves the right to cancel the test taker's score."

### Inferences
- ETS's dictated-scratchpad rule is a useful precedent for a voice assistant. The assistant may **store and repeat what the student dictates** (intermediate results) without computing or checking anything. That supports working memory without giving a hint.
- College Board's secure, scripted reader means its reader wording is controlled at item level. This matches the inference from Q2 that a machine reader needs per-item scripts or flags.

### Gaps
- College Board's reader script and ACT's reader manual are not public. No primary "may/may not" text from either was found.
- The ETS document dates from 2002. No newer ETS version was found.

## Q4. Turkey: ÖSYM, the 2022 Regulation, MEB (LGS, Merkezî Sistem Sınav Yönergesi, Özel Eğitim Hizmetleri Yönetmeliği)

### Takeaway
- No Turkish document read contains an itemized list of what the reader may and may not say, and none uses the words "açıklama" (explanation), "yorum" (interpretation) or "ipucu" (hint).
- The binding general rule is **Article 11 of the 2022 Regulation**. Readers and markers sign an undertaking that they will give "aday veya öğrenciye herhangi bir yardımda bulunmayacaklarına" ("no help of any kind to the candidate or student"). If they do, the candidate's exam is void and the official faces proceedings.
- Turkey handles visual content by **changing the question pool, not by describing it**:
  - ÖSYM does not ask reader-using blind candidates questions with figures, graphs, tables, pictures, or complex expressions.
  - MEB LGS substitutes equivalent questions.
- MEB school-level assessment does allow figure questions to be given "betimlenerek" ("by describing them").
- Since 2026, MEB requires readers and coders to hold a course certificate that includes "betimleyici okuma teknikleri" ("descriptive reading techniques"). The course content is not public.

### Inferences
- **The Turkish boundary in one sentence:** the reader reads the questions and warnings, on request (Regulation Art. 3; AİS guide; MEB Yönerge Art. 22). The reader gives "herhangi bir yardım" ("help of any kind") under penalty of voiding the exam (Art. 11). Visual items are removed or replaced, not interpreted (ÖSYM AİS; LGS §8.1). This is broader but vaguer than JCQ's "must not explain or clarify". Turkey forbids *any* help and does not enumerate what counts as help.
- **For a Turkish voice assistant, borrow the itemized lists from abroad:**
  - JCQ 5.5: read, repeat on request, spell, decode only if approved, never explain or clarify.
  - Smarter Balanced and South Dakota: no paraphrase, interpretation, definition or translation.
  - Pennsylvania and Louisiana: no cueing by inflection; no solving while reading.
  - STA: no symbol names, no terminology explanation; refusal script "I cannot tell you, but think hard and try to remember".
  - Cite Regulation Art. 11 as the Turkish legal anchor.
- **Figures:** ÖSYM's approach implies there is no sanctioned Turkish protocol for describing figures in its central exams. But MEB's Special Education Regulation does allow "betimleme" (description) in school assessment, and MEB's 2026 course teaches "betimleyici okuma" (descriptive reading). For school or LGS-style practice, neutral description is consistent with MEB policy. Use the Louisiana rule "Describe only what is seen … do not provide interpretation" and the JCQ pentagon test.
- **Dictated operations:** the marker in the ÖSYM directive "adayın söylediği işlemleri de yapma" ("also carries out the operations the candidate dictates"), and ETS allows the same. This officially sanctions a mode where the assistant records or repeats the student's dictated steps without evaluating them.
- **Reader qualification:** "matematiksel ve sayısal ifadeleri okuyabilen" ("able to read mathematical and numerical expressions"; Art. 11) implies the expectation of accurate math verbalization, as in JCQ's "decode symbols and formulae with accuracy". No Turkish standard specifies *how* (e.g., "eksi" (minus) vs "negatif" (negative)).

### Gaps
- **No Turkish primary text was found that explicitly says the reader may not explain, interpret, paraphrase or give hints (açıklama/yorum/ipucu).** The closest is the Art. 11 undertaking "herhangi bir yardımda bulunmayacaklarına" ("that they will not give help of any kind"). The text of the ÖSYM reader undertaking form (taahhüt belgesi) and any ÖSYM or MEB "okuyucu-kodlayıcı görev talimatı" (reader-coder duty instruction) were not found online.
- MEB's "Sınav Tedbir Hizmeti Uygulayıcı Kursu" curriculum ("betimleyici okuma teknikleri") is not public. It may contain the only Turkish reader do/don't list.
- A search summary claimed that "görevi sırasında engelli adaylara destek oldukları tespit edilen görevlilere daha sonraki sınavlarda görev verilmez" ("officials found to have helped candidates with disabilities during their duty are not given duties in later exams"). It was **not verified** in any full text read [ÖZET]. It possibly comes from ÖSYM's rules on selecting exam officials.
- The MEB Ölçme ve Değerlendirme Yönetmeliği (RG 9/9/2023, No. 32304) itself was not read. mevzuat.gov.tr returned a script page for the guessed ID.
- No official Turkish math verbalization standard (negatif/eksi, kesir okuma = fraction reading) was found.
